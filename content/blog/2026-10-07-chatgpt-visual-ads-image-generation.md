Ads in ChatGPT are about to get pictures. On Monday, October 5, OpenAI said it will test a visual ad format that shows up while ChatGPT is generating an image: the few seconds when you're waiting for a result, and often still deciding what you want.

It's a small test with a big audience behind it. OpenAI told developers in late September that ChatGPT now reaches about 1.2 billion people a week, up from roughly 900 million in February, [according to The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-now-reaches-1-2-billion-people-every-week-openai-says/).

## How the new ads work

The test starts later this month in the US, on ChatGPT's free tier, with an initial group of advertisers. The ads can use imagery to show product inspiration, how a product is used, or an experience tied to a service.

Two rules matter for users. The ads are labeled, and they sit beside the generated image rather than inside it. Ask for a sketch of a reading nook and the picture you get isn't shaped by whoever bought the slot; the sponsored card appears next to it.

This builds on the ad test OpenAI started on February 9, 2026, for logged-in adults on the Free and Go plans in the US. Paid plans, including Plus and Pro, remain ad-free, and OpenAI has said ads don't influence ChatGPT's answers.

## The part advertisers will care about more

The format got the headlines, but the bigger change for marketers is measurement. Alongside the new ads, OpenAI widened the set of tools advertisers can use to check whether ChatGPT campaigns work. Attribution partners now include AppsFlyer, Adjust, Branch, Kochava and Triple Whale, [according to Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/openai-introduces-visual-chatgpt-ad-format-and-expanded-measurement/), which covers both web and app campaigns.

OpenAI is also running brand-suitability pilots with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science, two of the largest ad-verification firms, which advertisers use to keep their ads away from content they don't want to appear next to. Under the pilots, they'll evaluate how OpenAI applies its brand-safety standards in a controlled environment, without access to users' private conversations.

That combination, a new placement plus third-party measurement and safety checks, is what ad buyers usually wait for before they move real budget onto a new platform.

## Why image generation is valuable ad space

Asking ChatGPT to picture a mid-century living room, a wedding cake or a garden layout says a lot about what someone wants, and sometimes about what they're about to buy. That's the kind of intent Pinterest built its ad business on: people browsing ideas before they shop.

Placing a relevant product image next to a generated one is a natural fit for furniture, fashion, travel and home brands. It's also a test of user patience: the ad sits right beside something the user just made, and how people react there will shape how far OpenAI takes the format.

## What it means for marketers and site owners

- **A new channel is forming.** With labeled formats, attribution partners and brand-safety pilots, ChatGPT ads look less like an experiment and more like a platform that will compete with search and social for budget.
- **Visual brands should get ready.** If the test opens up, the advertisers who benefit first will be the ones with strong product imagery and landing pages that load fast and match the ad.
- **Organic discovery keeps shifting.** More of the moments that used to start with a search now start in an AI assistant, and those moments are now being sold. Clear product pages and structured data, the kind of things search engines and AI tools can read easily, are worth investing in either way.

## What to watch

- When the test opens to more advertisers, and whether it expands beyond the US.
- Whether the visual format moves into ChatGPT's regular answers, not just image generation.
- How users react. OpenAI has promised clear labels and separation; the test will show whether that's enough to keep free-tier users comfortable.
